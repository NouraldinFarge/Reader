# AI assistance and human accountability

Reader was developed with substantial AI-agent assistance. The disclosure is part of the engineering record, not a substitute for authorship or review.

AI coding agents assisted with research, implementation, testing, and iteration. Nouraldin Farge defined the requirements and architecture, reviewed and validated changes, set the safety, licensing, and data-source boundaries, and retains responsibility for published claims and releases.

The owner-confirmed origin and repository-history context are recorded in [Project provenance](PROVENANCE.md).

## Accountability model

- Human requirements and acceptance criteria define the product scope, including the no-DRM, privacy, one-window, no-terminal, licensing, signing, and publication boundaries.
- Generated code and recommendations are treated as untrusted proposals and checked against source, tests, runtime behavior, official dependency material, and artifact hashes.
- Human approval is required for licensing, signing-certificate use, repository creation/visibility, remotes, pushes, tags, releases, deployments, profile edits, résumé changes, posts, and applications.
- Test automation demonstrates specific observed behavior; it does not prove universal safety, accessibility, compatibility, or correctness.
- The facts ledger records limitations and failed gates as carefully as passing evidence.
- Commit messages must describe actual work and must not invent a conventional team or backdated development history.

Any future public release should preserve the concise README disclosure and retain this detailed record in the repository.
